SLIDE 2 - BACKGROUND 1: WHY AI LITERACY MATTERS
============================================================

Slide purpose:
Explain the educational problem briefly: students need AI literacy, not only digital literacy.

Layout:
Two-column layout. Left side for short problem bullets, right side for the AI literacy reference figure.

On-slide content:
- AI is increasingly present in learning, search, recommendation systems, and daily digital activities.
- Digital literacy is no longer enough; students also need to understand how AI produces outputs.
- AI literacy includes understanding AI, using AI appropriately, evaluating AI outputs, and recognizing ethical implications.
- Junior high school students need concrete learning experiences because AI concepts are often abstract.

Visual asset:
image/ppt_12_slides/slide_02_ai_literacy_framework.png

Visual caption:
AI literacy dimensions adapted from Ng et al.

Speaker notes:
The first issue is that AI is no longer a distant technology. Students interact with intelligent systems through many daily platforms. However, if AI literacy is taught only as theory, students may know the terms but still fail to evaluate AI outputs critically. This project responds to that gap by designing an interactive learning experience.

SLIDE 4 - PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OBJECTIVES
============================================================

Slide purpose:
Summarize the research questions and objective in a concise form.

Layout:
Three-block layout: Problem, Research Questions, Objective.

On-slide content:
Problem:
- Students may treat AI output as an absolute answer.
- AI prediction is probabilistic, but this is not always visible to students.
- AI literacy needs a medium that allows students to evaluate AI output directly.

Research questions:
- How can a level-based MediaPipe finger tracking simulation provide a concrete AI literacy experience for junior high school students?
- How can Human-in-the-Loop be integrated through Top-3 prediction, confidence score, and student decision options?
- How can interaction data be used to observe students' decision patterns toward AI output?

Objective:
- To produce an interactive AI literacy prototype where students draw, read AI predictions, validate the output, and observe the gameplay consequences of their decisions.

SLIDE 5 - PROPOSED LEARNING INTERACTION
============================================================

Slide purpose:
Explain the core loop of the proposed system before entering technical architecture.

Layout:
Horizontal process layout with five steps.

On-slide content:
Core interaction loop:
1. Student draws an object using finger movement.
2. The sketch is processed by the AI classifier.
3. The system displays Top-3 prediction and confidence score.
4. Student chooses Accept, Correct, Override, or Redraw.
5. The final decision becomes an object behavior in the 2D simulation.

Key message:
- AI acts as a prediction assistant.
- The student remains the creator, evaluator, and final decision maker.
- Gameplay consequences make AI validation visible and concrete.

Visual asset:
image/ppt_12_slides/slide_05_06_system_architecture.png

Visual note:
Use the global system diagram as a reference image, or crop the Input-Process-Output flow if the slide needs more space.

Speaker notes:
This slide explains the learning loop. The system does not stop after giving a prediction. Instead, the prediction becomes a decision point. When students accept, correct, reject, or redraw, they learn that AI output requires validation before it is used.

SLIDE 6 - GLOBAL SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

Slide purpose:
Show the complete technical structure of the proposed solution.

Layout:
Full-width architecture diagram with three short callouts below.

On-slide content:
Architecture layers:
- Input: camera, finger tracking, drawing canvas, and student decision.
- Process Layer 1: interaction and 2D simulation.
- Process Layer 2: AI classification using CNN/MobileNet and TensorFlow.js.
- Process Layer 3: Human-in-the-Loop decision resolver.
- Process Layer 4: interaction log and dashboard data.
- Output: Top-3 prediction, confidence score, gameplay consequence, and analytics data.

Visual asset:
image/ppt_12_slides/slide_05_06_system_architecture.png

Visual caption:
Global architecture of the proposed AI literacy simulation.

Speaker notes:
The architecture separates the system into input, process, and output. The important point is the decision layer. The AI prediction is not directly executed by the game. It first passes through Human-in-the-Loop validation, where the student's decision determines the final object label and behavior.

SLIDE 9 - HITL DECISION LAYER AND INTERACTION TYPE
============================================================

Slide purpose:
Explain how the student decision becomes part of the system logic.

Layout:
Interaction matrix as the main visual. Add a compact decision explanation beside it.

On-slide content:
Decision options:
- Accept: student accepts the first prediction as the final label.
- Correct: student selects another prediction from the Top-3 list.
- Override: student rejects all predictions and manually determines the label.
- Redraw: student decides the sketch is unclear and repeats the drawing process.

Object mapping:
- Valid final label can become a Solid object that helps level progress.
- Incorrect or risky mapping can become a Danger object or trigger recovery.
- The decision is stored as interaction data for analysis.

Visual asset:
image/ppt_12_slides/slide_09_interaction_type_matrix.png

Visual caption:
Interaction type matrix for input, AI response, decision, and system feedback.

Speaker notes:
This slide is the core of the Human-in-the-Loop mechanism. AI gives a prediction, but the student controls the final decision. The system then translates that decision into gameplay behavior. This makes validation more concrete than a normal quiz or static explanation.
