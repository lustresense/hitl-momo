Project Context

Why This Project Is Needed

AI literacy should not only teach students how to use AI, but also how to evaluate AI output.

Problem Impact on Students Proposed Direction
AI output is often treated as a final answer. Students may accept AI without evaluation. Show AI prediction as uncertain output.
AI concepts are abstract for younger learners. Students may not understand confidence and error. Use drawing-based interactive simulation.
Learning media often lacks decision consequence. Students only observe, not decide. Add a Human-in-the-Loop decision moment.
AIis introduced as a probabilistic system.
HITLkeeps students as active evaluators.
Gameturns decisions into visible consequences.
Logrecords interaction patterns for review.
AI literacy dimensions used as the educational foundation.

Level Interaction Design

Progressive AI Uncertainty Across Levels

Each level introduces a different intensity of AI uncertainty and student decision-making.

Level Interaction Focus AI Display Student Task Main Learning Moment
Level 1 Guided recognition Top-1 only Draw and accept a simple object. Understand that AI can classify sketches.
Level 2 Ambiguous prediction Top-3 + confidence Compare prediction alternatives. Learn that AI output has uncertainty.
Level 3 Critical decision Confident but wrong prediction Override or redraw. Learn that humans must validate AI.
1

Guided

Students learn the basic loop with low ambiguity and simple feedback.

2

Ambiguous

Students compare alternatives and interpret confidence values.

3

Critical

Students challenge AI output when the prediction does not match their intention.

System Hierarchy

Interaction First, Education Through Play

The mascot bridges the system hierarchy so students learn from the experience, not from a lecture.

Interaction

Natural entry point through camera, gesture, drawing canvas, and simple UI.

Gameplay & Storyline

A simple puzzle-platformer context turns drawing and prediction into a playable goal.

Education

AI literacy appears through reflection on prediction, confidence, correction, and consequence.

Mascot as Bridge

A visual companion that softens instruction, signals uncertainty, and connects game feedback to AI literacy.

Design Principle

Teach without making students feel lectured.

Interaction lowers friction so students can start playing before facing theory.
Gameplay gives students a reason to care about their AI decision.
The mascot turns prediction moments into short cues, warnings, and reflections.
Education is placed at the end of the loop, after students experience the consequence.
