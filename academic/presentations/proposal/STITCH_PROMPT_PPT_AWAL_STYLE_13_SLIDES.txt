Slide 2 - Project Context
Heading:
Why This Project Is Needed
Subheading:
AI literacy should not only teach students how to use AI, but also how to evaluate AI output.
Content cards:
1. AI output is often treated as a final answer.
   Impact: students may accept AI without evaluation.
   Direction: show AI prediction as uncertain output.
2. AI concepts are abstract.
   Impact: students may not understand confidence and error.
   Direction: use drawing-based interactive simulation.
3. Existing learning media often lacks decision consequence.
   Impact: students only observe, not decide.
   Direction: add Human-in-the-Loop decision moment.
Tiny note:
Inspired by exploratory AI literacy approaches for younger learners, but implemented through visual classification and gameplay decisions.

Slide 3 - Proposed Solution
Heading:
Proposed Solution
Subheading:
A web-based interactive simulation where students draw objects, inspect AI predictions, and decide the final object behavior.
Main points:
- Students draw objects through a canvas-based interaction.
- The system displays AI prediction through a Top-3 UI.
- Students choose to accept, correct, override, or redraw.
- The decision affects object behavior inside the 2D game.
- Interaction data is logged for decision-pattern analysis.
Visual:
Friendly flow diagram:
Student Drawing -> AI Prediction -> Top-3 UI -> Human Decision -> 2D Gameplay Consequence -> Interaction Log

Slide 9 - Level Interaction Design
Heading:
Level Interaction Design
Subheading:
Each level introduces a different intensity of AI uncertainty and student decision-making.
Content:
- Level 1: Guided recognition. Top-1 only. Draw and accept simple object. Learn that AI can classify sketches.
- Level 2: Ambiguous prediction. Top-3 + confidence. Compare prediction alternatives. Learn that AI output has uncertainty.
- Level 3: Critical decision. Confident but wrong prediction. Override or redraw. Learn that humans must validate AI.
